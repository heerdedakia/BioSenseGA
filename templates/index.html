About BioSenseGA

Bio-inspired Artificial Nose Sensor Array Optimization & Machine Learning Classification

Project Overview & Motivation

BioSenseGA is an advanced engineering platform that simulates the biological olfactory system using an array of 16 Figaro metal oxide semiconductor (MOX) gas sensors. In real-world artificial olfaction, MOX sensor arrays suffer from chemical sensor drift caused by sensor aging, temperature fluctuations, and environmental contamination over extended operational periods.

Furthermore, operating 16 physical sensors with 128 extracted transient dynamics features imposes high power consumption, computational overhead, and multicollinearity. BioSenseGA addresses this challenge by employing a Genetic Algorithm (GA) to search the combinatorial space of \(2^{128}\) possible feature subsets, identifying the minimal salient sensor feature subset that preserves or improves gas classification accuracy with a Random Forest ensemble.

This project is designed as an Engineering Lab & Demonstration system carrying 40% of the course grade, demonstrating the synergy of bio-inspired optimization and ensemble machine learning for industrial and environmental sensing.

Feature Extraction Dynamics

Each sensor produces 8 engineered features: steady-state differential resistance (\(\Delta R\)), absolute magnitude (\(|\Delta R|\)), and 6 exponential moving averages (EMA) across 3 scalar smoothing factors (\(\alpha = 0.001, 0.01, 0.1\)) capturing rising adsorption and decaying desorption phases.

Genetic Optimization Formulation

Binary chromosome with 128 loci. Fitness function balances cross-validation accuracy and a parsimony penalty:
Fitness = Accuracy - λ × (N_selected / 128). Employs tournament selection (\(k=3\)), single-point crossover, bit-flip mutation, and strict elitism.

Scientific Validation Protocol

Adheres to strict anti-leakage methodology: stratified 80/20 train/test split, StandardScaler fitted strictly on training data, GA surrogate cross-validation executed solely within the training partition, and final evaluation conducted on held-out test data.
